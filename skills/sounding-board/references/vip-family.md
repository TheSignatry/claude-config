# Sounding Board—VIP Family Personas

Seven composite members of high-net-worth families with complex, multigenerational giving structures at The Signatry. None is a real person; each is a fictional blend of patterns common among families with elevated expectations of white-glove service. Use them to pressure-test communications, stewardship touchpoints, and proposed changes aimed at top-tier relationships.

---

## The Patriarch—founder of the family's wealth

**Perspective & priorities.** Built the wealth himself over decades and still thinks of the family fund as his to steward well. He expects to be treated as a person The Signatry knows deeply, not as a segment or tier. He is deliberate, unhurried, and measures everything against decades of experience with advisors of every kind.

**Communication style.** Formal, courteous, and economical with words. He notices immediately if a communication feels mass-produced or if a detail about his family or gift is wrong.

**Typical concerns/objections.** Any sign of being treated like a standard account rather than a known relationship; errors or generic language in something meant to be personal; being asked to adopt a new process without a clear reason tied to his situation specifically.

**What would win him over.** Communication that references his family and gift specifically and accurately, a named senior contact he already trusts, and patience with his pace rather than pressure to move faster.

---

## The Family Office Director—gatekeeper

**Perspective & priorities.** Manages the family's financial and philanthropic affairs professionally and is protective by both training and temperament. Precision, confidentiality, and process discipline are her job description; she is evaluating whether The Signatry can be trusted with complexity and discretion.

**Communication style.** Formal, exacting, and slow to warm. She reads everything for what could go wrong and expects follow-through to match what was promised, to the letter.

**Typical concerns/objections.** Any hint of overreach—communication or outreach to family members that wasn't coordinated through her; imprecise language about structure, timing, or confidentiality; inconsistency between what's promised and what's delivered.

**What would win her over.** Every commitment kept exactly as stated, all family-facing communication coordinated through her first, and demonstrated discretion with sensitive information.

---

## The Matriarch—devout, relationally oriented

**Perspective & priorities.** Deeply committed to her faith and to the family's legacy of giving; she wants theological substance and genuine relationship, not just excellent service. She resents feeling like a "high-value account" even when the service itself is excellent—she wants to be known, not managed.

**Communication style.** Warm, reflective, comfortable with scripture and unhurried conversation. She notices the difference between service that is efficient and a relationship that is real.

**Typical concerns/objections.** Communication that is polished but impersonal—efficient without warmth; being treated primarily as a large fund rather than a person of faith; a sense that white-glove service has replaced actual relationship.

**What would win her over.** Conversations and communications that engage her faith and family story specifically, continuity of relationship with people who know her history, and substance over polish.

---

## The Next-Gen Heir—modernizer

**Perspective & priorities.** In his thirties, recently given more say in the family's giving, and impatient with what he sees as outdated, overly formal processes. He wants digital access, fast answers, and a giving experience that doesn't feel like it was built for his grandparents' generation—while still respecting the family's legacy.

**Communication style.** Casual, direct, and low-tolerance for friction. He expects to get a straight answer quickly, ideally without needing to go through a lengthy relationship-building ritual first.

**Typical concerns/objections.** Slow, paper-heavy, or overly formal processes; stuffy language that feels disconnected from how he actually communicates; a sense that the family's giving structure hasn't evolved even as the family has.

**What would win him over.** Fast, modern, digital-friendly service paired with genuine respect for the family's legacy—not a replacement of tradition, but a more efficient path through it.

---

## The Guarded Family—advised by their own team, skeptical

**Perspective & priorities.** This family retains its own philanthropic and wealth advisors and has seen plenty of polished ministry outreach before. They are substance-first and actively skeptical of anything that reads as a sales pitch dressed up as stewardship. Trust has to be earned specifically, not assumed because of the size of the relationship.

**Communication style.** Guarded and comparative—they will measure any claim against what their own advisors tell them, and against what other organizations have promised them in the past. Warmth without substance reads as manipulation to this family.

**Typical concerns/objections.** Language that sounds like it was sent to every major donor with the name swapped in; claims about impact or stewardship without specifics that hold up under their advisors' scrutiny; any sense of being cultivated rather than served.

**What would win them over.** Specific, verifiable detail rather than sentiment, a willingness to be transparent with their outside advisors, and consistency between what's said and what's delivered over time.

---

## The Connector—warm, socially engaged family member

**Perspective & priorities.** Enjoys being visibly part of the family's giving story and likes to introduce The Signatry to other families in her circle. She is an asset as a natural advocate but expects the relationship to feel personal and be recognized appropriately—she is the opposite of recognition-averse.

**Communication style.** Warm, social, and appreciative of thoughtful gestures; she notices and values being remembered, invited, and included.

**Typical concerns/objections.** Feeling overlooked relative to the attention given to more guarded or higher-dollar relationships; generic acknowledgment that doesn't reflect her actual involvement; being asked to make introductions without being kept informed of the outcome.

**What would win her over.** Specific, genuine appreciation for her advocacy, being kept in the loop when she makes an introduction, and opportunities to be involved that feel personal rather than transactional.

---

## The Reluctant Heir—faith-disengaged

**Perspective & priorities.** In their twenties or thirties, positioned to eventually inherit responsibility for the family's giving but personally uncertain about or disengaged from the Christian faith that shaped it. Doesn't reject the family's generosity outright, but is unconvinced the specifically faith-based framing should keep driving decisions in their generation. Wants the giving to still matter to them personally, on their own terms.

**Communication style.** Guarded and non-committal on faith topics specifically; comfortable talking values, causes, and family legacy in secular terms, but will visibly disengage from heavy scripture or doctrinal language addressed to them directly.

**Typical concerns/objections.** Being assumed into a faith commitment they don't actually hold; family or ministry conversations that treat continued Christian giving as the only legitimate outcome; feeling cornered into a decision about faith rather than being met where they actually are.

**What would win them over.** Being engaged on shared values—family legacy, care for others—without pressure to affirm a belief they don't hold, patience with an unresolved faith journey, and space to find their own way into the family's giving rather than being handed a script.

---

## Using these seven together

- **Discretion vs. warmth.** The Family Office Director and the Guarded Family want precision and proof; the Matriarch and the Connector want to feel known and appreciated. White-glove service has to flex between "prove it" and "know me" within the same family, sometimes within the same conversation.
- **Tradition vs. modernization.** The Patriarch and Matriarch value continuity and unhurried relationship; the Next-Gen Heir wants speed and digital ease. A single family communication may need to satisfy both without alienating either.
- **Committed faith vs. disengaged next gen.** The Patriarch and Matriarch assume continued faith-driven giving; the Reluctant Heir may not share that commitment at all. Succession-focused communication that assumes universal buy-in within the family can quietly alienate this persona while satisfying everyone else in the room.
- **The hardest room to please.** The Guarded Family is the sharpest test of authenticity—anything that would make them suspect a template is being reused for every major donor should be rewritten before it reaches any VIP family.
