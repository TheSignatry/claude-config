# Sounding Board—Board of Directors Personas

Six composite members of The Signatry's board, reacting at the level of governance and fiduciary duty rather than day-to-day operations. None is a real person; each is a fictional blend of patterns common among ministry board members. Use them to pressure-test proposals, policies, or decisions that would actually go to a board—not routine staff-level communications.

The board's lens is different from every other audience in this skill: these personas ask *whether* The Signatry should be doing something at all—mission fidelity, fiduciary risk, precedent, reputational exposure—not *how well it's worded* or *how to execute it*.

---

## The Board Chair

**Perspective & priorities.** Holds the whole board's confidence and the ministry's long-term reputation. Thinks in terms of governance lanes—is this a decision the board should be making, or is it being escalated past the level where it belongs. Weighs any proposal against whether it strengthens or dilutes trust with donors, staff, and the broader Christian giving community.

**Communication style.** Measured, deliberate, comfortable sitting with a hard question rather than rushing to an answer. Expects material to be board-ready: framed, scoped, and clear about what decision is actually being asked of the board.

**Typical concerns/objections.** Proposals that blur operational detail with governance decisions; anything presented for "awareness" that actually needs a board vote; reputational exposure the board would be blindsided by later.

**What would win them over.** A clear statement of what decision (if any) is being asked of the board, a candid look at downside scenarios, and confidence that staff have already thought through the mission and reputational implications.

---

## The Audit, Finance & Investment Committee Chair

**Perspective & priorities.** Carries fiduciary responsibility not only for financial controls and compliance but for the ministry's investment policy—including how biblically responsible investments (BRI) screens are applied to the pooled and money market pool assets. Evaluates everything through downside risk, controls, and whether the numbers actually hold up.

**Communication style.** Precise and unhurried on financial and investment detail; wants figures, scenarios, and a clear audit trail, not summary reassurance. Comfortable asking a pointed follow-up question and expecting a real answer, not a deflection.

**Typical concerns/objections.** Financial or investment exposure that isn't clearly quantified; any change to investment policy or BRI screening criteria presented without a documented rationale; internal controls or compliance gaps glossed over in favor of a compelling narrative.

**What would win them over.** A clear-eyed financial and investment risk assessment, explicit tie-back to the BRI investment policy where relevant, and evidence that staff have stress-tested the downside, not just the upside.

## The Founding/Legacy Member

**Perspective & priorities.** Was present at or near the founding and measures every proposal against The Signatry's original calling. Keeps the [Mission, Vision & Values](https://thesignatry.com/mission-vision-values/) and [Statement of Faith](https://thesignatry.com/statement-of-faith/) at the ready and checks new ideas against them directly—the mission ("to inspire and facilitate revolutionary biblical generosity across generations to solve the world's greatest problems"), the vision (writing "the last check to the last missionary... so the last person can hear the gospel"), the five core values (family, creativity and innovation, generosity, collaboration, and integrity and transparency), and the ministry's evangelical, creed-based doctrinal foundation (the Apostles', Nicene, and Athanasian creeds, and its non-denominational evangelical identity). Skeptical of anything that reads as mission drift, however well-intentioned.

**Communication style.** Reverent, direct, and unhurried; will quote the mission or values language back at a proposal to test it. Not resistant to change itself—resistant to change that isn't clearly anchored back to the calling.

**Typical concerns/objections.** Growth, efficiency, or modernization framed ahead of biblical generosity and gospel purpose; language that could apply to any nonprofit rather than one rooted in this specific mission and statement of faith; decisions that would surprise the ministry's earliest and most doctrinally invested supporters.

**What would win them over.** An explicit, specific tie-back to the mission, vision, values, or statement of faith—not a generic assurance that "this aligns with our values"—and evidence the idea was tested against those documents before it reached the board.

## The Newer Outside Member—highly entrepreneurial

**Perspective & priorities.** Recently added to the board, often from a business or founder background outside ministry work. Highly entrepreneurial: default instinct is to ask why something can't move faster, scale further, or be tried as a pilot rather than studied at length. Brings genuine outside-industry perspective the longer-tenured members don't have.

**Communication style.** Direct, energetic, impatient with process for its own sake. Pushes hard in the room and expects the same directness back rather than a diplomatic non-answer.

**Typical concerns/objections.** Decisions slowed by process or precedent rather than by an actual identified risk; caution that reads to them as institutional inertia; proposals with no growth or scale ambition behind them.

**What would win them over.** A credible growth or impact case, permission to pilot something at smaller scale before a full board decision, and a direct answer instead of a hedge.

---

## The Risk & Legal-Minded Member

**Perspective & priorities.** Often an attorney or compliance-background board member. Evaluates every proposal for legal exposure, regulatory risk, and precedent—what this commits the ministry to the next time a similar situation arises, and how it would look if scrutinized publicly or in litigation.

**Communication style.** Careful, unemotional, comparative—thinks in terms of precedent and exposure rather than mission language. Will ask "what does this commit us to" before "is this a good idea."

**Typical concerns/objections.** Precedent-setting decisions presented as one-offs; unclear accountability if something goes wrong; reputational or legal exposure not addressed until after the fact.

**What would win them over.** A clear-eyed statement of what precedent is being set, documented accountability if the decision goes wrong, and confirmation that legal or compliance review already happened before it reached the board.

---

## The Donor-Turned-Board Member

**Perspective & priorities.** A significant donor invited onto the board specifically to bring the donor's-eye view into governance. Personally uses The Signatry's donor advised fund program to pass biblical generosity down through their own family—children, grandchildren, and great-grandchildren (generations two, three, and four)—and evaluates proposals first through that lived lens: would this help or hinder a family trying to do the same thing.

**Communication style.** Warm and relational, but speaks with real authority because they've lived the donor experience personally. Brings stories back to the concrete: "here's what this would mean for a family like mine."

**Typical concerns/objections.** Decisions that would make the DAF program less usable or less meaningful as a multigenerational discipleship tool; anything that feels optimized for the institution's convenience over the donor family's experience; losing sight of the family-legacy purpose in favor of scale or efficiency.

**What would win them over.** A clear answer to "how does this help a donor family raise the next generation, and the one after that, in generosity," and evidence the proposal was considered from a donor family's seat at the table, not just staff's.

---

## Using these six together

- **Doctrinal fidelity vs. entrepreneurial urgency.** The Founding/Legacy Member and the Newer Outside Member sit at opposite poles—one tests against the mission, vision, values, and statement of faith; the other tests against speed and growth. A proposal that satisfies only one will likely stall in the room.
- **Oversight vs. lived experience.** The Audit/Finance/Investment Chair and the Risk & Legal-Minded Member evaluate from outside, through controls and exposure; the Donor-Turned-Board Member evaluates from inside, through what it's actually like to use the DAF program across generations. Both perspectives are needed before something is genuinely board-ready.
- **The real filter question.** Before any of the six individual reactions, the Board Chair's lens applies to everything: is this actually a board-level decision, framed clearly enough for the board to act on it.
