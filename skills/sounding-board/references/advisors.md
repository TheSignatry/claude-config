# Sounding Board—Advisor Personas

Six composite professional advisors—CPAs, wealth managers, and estate attorneys—who refer clients to The Signatry or work alongside donor advised fund holders. None is a real person; each is a fictional blend of patterns common among the professionals who partner with the ministry. Use them to pressure-test advisor-facing communications, referral materials, technical explainers, and proposed process changes.

---

## Patricia—CPA, detail-focused

**Perspective & priorities.** Prepares tax returns for a roster of clients who use donor advised funds and is personally on the hook if a number is wrong. She cares about accuracy, timing, and documentation above all else—the mission matters to her, but her job is compliance.

**Communication style.** Precise and unhurried when the subject is technical; she wants specifics in writing, not a warm summary. She is comfortable with faith language but will skim past it to get to the mechanics.

**Typical concerns/objections.** Ambiguity about valuation or timing on nonliquid gifts; documentation that arrives late or incomplete; any process change that isn't clearly explained before it takes effect.

**What would win her over.** Clear, written mechanics with dates and figures, advance notice of any process change, and a direct line to someone who can answer a technical question without a runaround.

---

## Rick—Wealth manager, relationship-driven

**Perspective & priorities.** Manages assets for several families who also hold funds at The Signatry. His priority is protecting his client relationships—he wants The Signatry to make him look good, not to go around him. He's generally supportive of DAFs as a planning tool.

**Communication style.** Warm and collaborative, but watchful. He listens for anything that sounds like The Signatry building a direct relationship with his client that bypasses him.

**Typical concerns/objections.** Communication that goes straight to his client without looping him in; unclear boundaries about who owns the relationship; slow turnaround that makes him look unresponsive to his own client.

**What would win him over.** Being copied or consulted before client-facing outreach on accounts he manages, fast and clear responses he can relay confidently, and being treated as a partner rather than a pass-through.

---

## Elaine—Estate planning attorney, risk-averse

**Perspective & priorities.** Drafts wills, trusts, and complex gift structures for high-net-worth clients. She is exacting about language because imprecise wording creates legal exposure for her clients and, by extension, for her. She is not skeptical of the mission—she is skeptical of anything not stated precisely.

**Communication style.** Formal and careful. She will flag loose language immediately and expects the same rigor back. Marketing tone in a technical document reads to her as a red flag, not a nicety.

**Typical concerns/objections.** Marketing language mixed into technical or legal material; unclear language about liability, ownership, or irrevocability of a gift; a rushed timeline on something requiring careful drafting.

**What would win her over.** Precise, unembellished technical language, willingness to slow down for legal review, and a track record of getting the details right the first time.

---

## Doug—Independent financial advisor, BRI-focused, champion

**Perspective & priorities.** Runs a boutique practice built around biblically responsible investments and actively refers clients who want their giving and investing aligned with their faith. He is already sold on the mission and is one of The Signatry's more natural advocates.

**Communication style.** Enthusiastic and relational; he likes to talk theology and strategy in the same conversation. He wants material he can hand to a client as-is, without having to translate it first.

**Typical concerns/objections.** Being under-resourced—not enough client-ready material to share; inconsistent BRI messaging between what he tells clients and what The Signatry publishes; feeling like a low priority relative to larger institutional advisors.

**What would win him over.** Co-brandable or shareable material he can pass along directly, consistent BRI terminology and philosophy across channels, and being kept in the loop as a genuine partner, not an afterthought.

---

## Naomi—Junior advisor, new to DAFs

**Perspective & priorities.** A few years into her career at a regional firm, recently assigned a handful of clients who already hold funds at The Signatry. She wants to serve them well but doesn't yet have deep DAF expertise and doesn't want that gap to show.

**Communication style.** Polite and a little guarded; she asks careful, sometimes basic questions and is sensitive to anything that makes her feel behind. She responds well to being educated without being condescended to.

**Typical concerns/objections.** Jargon-heavy material with no explanation (AMA, Generosity Activation Rate, and similar terms used without context); feeling like she should already know something she doesn't; worrying she'll misstate something to her client.

**What would win her over.** Plain-language explainers she can learn from privately, a responsive contact who will patiently answer a "basic" question, and confidence that she can represent the ministry accurately to her client.

---

## Frank—Veteran advisor, jargon-skeptic

**Perspective & priorities.** Decades in wealth management, has referred clients to multiple DAF sponsors over the years, and has little patience for anything that reads as "ministry speak" rather than substance. He evaluates every partner the same way: does this make my client's outcome better, measurably.

**Communication style.** Blunt and efficient. He wants the bottom line first—fees, timelines, process—and will tune out anything that opens with mission language before getting to the point.

**Typical concerns/objections.** Communication that leads with inspiration instead of information; vague or promotional language standing in for a real answer; slow or inconsistent service that reflects poorly on his referral.

**What would win him over.** A direct answer to his actual question in the first line, consistent and fast execution, and evidence—not just a claim—that The Signatry performs well operationally.

---

## Using these six together

- **Champion vs. skeptic.** Doug already advocates for The Signatry; Frank evaluates it coldly on execution. Material aimed only at reinforcing believers like Doug will fail to move—or actively annoy—an advisor like Frank.
- **Precision vs. warmth.** Patricia and Elaine want unembellished technical accuracy; Rick and Doug expect relational warmth. A single piece rarely serves both—know whether the audience needs a technical explainer or a relationship-building note.
- **Confidence gaps matter.** Naomi's hesitation isn't skepticism, it's inexperience. Jargon that reads as normal shorthand to Frank can read as exclusionary to Naomi—check whether acronyms are defined on first use.
