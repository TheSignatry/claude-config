# The Signatry — Asset Giving

**Owner:** Ben Martin, VP Marketing
**Review:** annually, or on any change to acceptance criteria
**Verified:** 2026-07-27 — Ben Martin, VP Marketing, direct statement

Fee tiers for nonliquid gifts are in `fees.md`. This file covers what is accepted and how the gift works.

## What is accepted

**Most asset types.** All business structures, real estate, and other nonliquid items. **The primary factor is gift value, not asset type.**

## The $1 million floor — nonliquid gifts

**The Signatry does not accept a nonliquid gift where the value of the gift to The Signatry is less than $1 million.**

Read that precisely: the threshold is the value of **the stake being given to The Signatry**, not the value of the whole asset. A donor giving a 10% interest in a business needs that 10% to be worth $1 million, not the business.

This is the first qualifying question in any nonliquid gift conversation. A business owner whose intended gift falls below it does not have a nonliquid gift path — cash or publicly traded securities after the sale, with the tax consequences that carry, or a different structure.

**Cryptocurrency and publicly traded securities are not subject to the limit.** No minimum applies to those.

## Timing — the constraint that governs everything

**The Signatry must receive its ownership stake before the rest of the asset is otherwise sold or liquidated.**

A gift made after a sale closes is a gift of cash proceeds, not a gift of the asset, and the donor has already realized the gain. Once a transaction is far enough along, the window is closed.

> **Do not attempt to state where the line falls.** How near a pending sale a gift can be made — letters of intent, signed agreements, binding commitments — is a tax question with real doctrine behind it and belongs on the CLO list in `../facts.md`. Content may say the gift must come *before* the sale. It may not characterize how late is too late.

## What happens after transfer

1. The Signatry receives its ownership stake. **It becomes The Signatry's. Full legal control.** Not held on the donor's behalf, not directed by the donor.
2. The Signatry generally liquidates when the remaining ownership liquidates.
3. Resulting dollars go into the related donor advised fund, **less the stakeholder grant**.

Point 3 clarifies the fee mechanics: the minimum stakeholder grant in `fees.md` is netted from proceeds. It is not invoiced separately.

## How the stakeholder grant is actually funded

`VERIFIED` — Ben Martin, 2026-07-27. **This is the part most likely to be mischaracterized, in either direction.**

The published fees page calls this a nonliquid gift *expense*. That framing is incomplete. How it is funded depends on the model:

### When a sale is a near-term reality

**The stakeholder grant is the first grant from the resulting donor advised fund.** It is effectively deducted from the gift to The Signatry.

**It is not an additional cost to the donor.** It is a reduction in the charitable total remaining for the donor's further grant recommendations. Nothing comes out of the donor's pocket; the dollars were already charitable dollars.

### When the model is to donate and hold rather than liquidate

**The donor funds the stakeholder grant.** Here it may be an additional cost.

Even then it is a donation to a 501(c)(3) nonprofit and **may be tax deductible.**

> Reproduce that hedge. "May be tax deductible" is the accurate formulation and it is not to be upgraded to "is." Deductibility for a specific donor is a tax question — CLO list in `../facts.md`.

### How to talk about it

| Do not say | Say |
|---|---|
| "A $5M gift costs the donor $150,000" | Where a sale is near-term, the first $150,000 granted from the fund goes to The Signatry; the remainder is available for the donor's recommendations |
| "There are no costs to giving a business interest" | The stakeholder grant applies to every nonliquid gift and should be shown |
| "The fee is 3%" | Avoid rate language. It invites comparison to an expense ratio, which is the wrong mental model in the near-term-sale case. |

## Scale of the stakeholder grant

Published tiers from `fees.md`, with the share of the gift each represents. **In the near-term-sale model these are shares of charitable dollars redirected, not donor expenses.**

| Gift value to The Signatry | Minimum stakeholder grant | Share of gift |
|---|---|---|
| $1,000,000 (the floor) | $50,000 | 5.0% |
| $2,000,000 | $50,000 | 2.5% |
| $5,000,000 | $150,000 | 3.0% |
| $10,000,000 | $175,000 | 1.75% |
| $50,000,000 | $300,000 | 0.6% |

The share is highest at the floor and falls as gift value rises. Gifts held beyond one year also carry the annualized management fee in `fees.md`, beginning in year two.

## Usage rules

- **Never model a nonliquid gift without the stakeholder grant, and never mischaracterize it.** A July 2026 draft deck omitted it entirely from a $5 million scenario. A subsequent correction in this file overstated it as a $150,000 donor cost. Both were wrong. Show it, and show it as the section above describes.
- **Qualify on the $1 million floor before building any nonliquid gift scenario**, including hypothetical ones. An illustrative example below the floor describes something The Signatry would decline.
- **Label illustrative figures on the artifact**, not only in conversation.
- Any external content here is financial content and carries the disclaimer in `../facts.md`.
