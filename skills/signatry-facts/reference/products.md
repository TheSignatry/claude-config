## Donor advised fund

`VERIFIED` — `AR-2025`, `WEB-DAF`.

A donor establishes a DAF with a qualified 501(c)(3) sponsor like The Signatry and makes charitable donations to the fund. **The sponsor has legal control over the fund** and gives the donor advisory privileges to recommend how those dollars are invested and granted to other nonprofits over time.

Accepts cash, publicly traded securities, real estate, business interests, cryptocurrency, and other nonliquid assets. Balances may be invested in biblically responsible investment pools, advisor managed accounts, or vetted alternative investments.

The Signatry vets each recommended recipient for values alignment and good legal standing before sending the grant.

## Designated fund

`VERIFIED` — `WEB-OTHER` (thesignatry.com/other-ways-to-give/) and `WEB-BLOG-2025LIMITS`.

**Grants from a designated fund can only be sent to a single charity, specified when the fund is opened.** A donor may open multiple designated funds to support multiple charities.

**The QCD case.** Donor advised funds cannot receive qualified charitable distributions from an IRA; a distribution from an IRA to a DAF is not tax-free. Designated funds are eligible. This makes them the tool for individuals over age 70½ who want to give instead of taking a required minimum distribution that would raise taxable income.

> QCD age thresholds, annual dollar limits, and RMD ages are federal tax rules that change, and are **not** Signatry facts. Do not state them from this file. Route to the CLO list in `../facts.md`.

## Charity fund

`VERIFIED` — Charity Fund: How It Works, version 202411, thesignatry.com/charity-fund-pdf/.

**A charity fund is opened by a nonprofit, not by a donor.** It lets the nonprofit's donors give stocks, bonds, and other publicly traded securities directly rather than only cash.

How it works, as published:

1. The donor initiates a gift of stocks, bonds, or other publicly traded securities to a charity fund with The Signatry
2. The gift is liquidated and proceeds are placed into the charity fund
3. Gifts to a charity fund are tax deductible as an irrevocable gift to The Signatry
4. While held, contributions may be invested in biblically responsible investment pools
5. When the nonprofit is ready, it recommends a grant to itself from the fund

Stated benefits: The Signatry handles receipting and paperwork for the donor; the donor takes the deduction in the year of the gift while the nonprofit may leave the gift invested; charity funds are approved to receive IRA charitable rollover gifts, unlike DAFs; free to open and requires no IRS paperwork.

**Nonprofit contact for charity funds: 913-308-1999 · nonprofitsuccess@thesignatry.com.** Distinct from the main line.

> The published sheet uses "charitable rollover" where other pages say "qualified charitable distribution." Same instrument, two names. Prefer QCD, spelled out on first use.

## Other giving structures

`VERIFIED` — `WEB-OTHER`.

**Life insurance**, two routes: name a donor advised fund as the beneficiary of a policy (changeable later), or name The Signatry as owner of the policy with the DAF as beneficiary.

## Statement of Faith

`VERIFIED` — thesignatry.com/statement-of-faith/, page modified 2025-12-17.

The Signatry is **not affiliated with any single church or denomination** and is a Christian organization established in the evangelical tradition of the Church. It affirms:

- The Apostles' Creed and the Nicene Creed
- The Athanasian Creed, for the doctrine of the Trinity and the nature of Jesus Christ
- The key documents of the Lausanne Movement — the Lausanne Covenant (1974), the Manila Manifesto (1989), the Cape Town Commitment (2010), and the Seoul Statement (2024)

The Statement of Faith is the screen behind biblically responsible investment pool selection: BRI pools hold companies whose core business lines do not conflict with biblical principles and The Signatry's Statement of Faith.
